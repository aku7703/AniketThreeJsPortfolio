The reminders you'll be given (Piazza @53, Prof. Higger)

Gradient rules: \(\nabla(a^\top x)=a,\ \ \nabla(x^\top A x)=(A+A^\top)x\)

Lagrange: a max or min of \(f(x)\) subject to \(g(x)=0\) solves \(\nabla f(x)=\lambda\nabla g(x)\) and \(g(x)=0\).

SVM labels \(y\in\{-1,+1\}\); \(\hat y=\operatorname{sign}(w\cdot x+b)\); the margin of \(x\) is \(\frac{w\cdot x+b}{\|w\|}\). The margin lines (edges of the street) are \(w\cdot x+b=\pm1\).

Soft-margin SVM: \(\min_{w,b,\xi}\ \tfrac12\|w\|^2+C\sum_i\xi_i\) subject to \(y_i(w\cdot x_i+b)\ge 1-\xi_i\) and \(\xi_i\ge0\) for every \(i\).

Its solution: \(w=\sum_i\alpha_i y_i x_i,\ \sum_i\alpha_i y_i=0,\ 0\le\alpha_i\le C\), and \(\alpha_i=0\) unless \(y_i(w\cdot x_i+b)\le1\).

Not on the sheet, so memorize these

  • Street width \(=\dfrac{2}{\|w\|}\). Each edge is \(\dfrac{1}{\|w\|}\) from the boundary.
  • Slack \(\xi_i=\max\big(0,\ 1-y_i(w\cdot x_i+b)\big)\), and the four cases: out, on, in, wrong.
  • \(\nabla\|x-p\|^2=2(x-p)\). This is the \(A=I\) case of the sheet's rule.
  • The hard-margin check: every \(y_i(w\cdot x_i+b)\ge1\) and every \(\alpha_i\ge0\).
Lesson 1 · Intuition

Why \(\nabla f=\lambda\nabla g\)

You walk along the constraint path \(g(x)=0\) and watch \(f\). Three facts give the whole method:

  1. Walking with \(\nabla f\), \(f\) goes up. Walking against it, \(f\) goes down. Walking perpendicular to it, \(f\) doesn't change. (The change along a unit direction \(u\) is \(u\cdot\nabla f\).)
  2. At a constrained max or min, a step along the path can't raise or lower \(f\). So \(\nabla f\) is perpendicular to the path there.
  3. \(\nabla g\) is always perpendicular to the path. \(g\) stays at 0 along it, so \(\nabla g\) can't have a component along it.

Both gradients are perpendicular to the path, and in 2-D only one direction is. So they lie on the same line: \(\nabla f=\lambda\nabla g\). \(\lambda\) is just the stretch factor from \(\nabla g\) to \(\nabla f\). It can be negative (the two point opposite ways) and it can be 0.

From lecture (Nov 3)

"This is probably the big one for today": you're at a constrained optimum when the gradient is orthogonal to the path you walk on.

He also said: stay in symbols (\(w, b, p\)) until the very end. It's less writing, and the work carries over when the numbers change.

Three things that confuse people

  • Why \(\|x\|^2\) instead of \(\|x\|\)? Both have the same minimizer, and the squared version has a clean gradient (\(2x\)). It's purely a convenience.
  • Why must \(g(x)=0\)? It's a convention. "\(x_0+2x_1=5\)" becomes \(g(x)=x_0+2x_1-5\). Moving the constant over changes nothing.
  • A negative \(\lambda\) is fine for an equality constraint (HW8 P1 ii has \(\lambda=-4\)). Only the SVM's \(\alpha_i\), which come from inequality constraints, must be \(\ge0\).

Lesson 2 · HW8 P1 and P2

The Lagrange recipe

Four steps

1. Write \(f\) and \(g\), with \(g(x)=0\).

2. Take both gradients (sheet rules, or partial derivatives).

3. From \(\nabla f=\lambda\nabla g\), solve for \(x\) in terms of \(\lambda\).

4. Substitute into \(g(x)=0\) to get \(\lambda\), then \(x\). Check: does \(x\) satisfy the constraint?

That gives 3 equations (2 from the vector equation, 1 from the constraint) in 3 unknowns \(x_0, x_1, \lambda\).

HW8 P1 i · The fence (max area)

\(f=x_0x_1\), \(g=x_0+2x_1-8\). \(\nabla f=(x_1,x_0)=\lambda(1,2)\), so \(x_1=\lambda\) and \(x_0=2\lambda\). Then \(2\lambda+2\lambda=8\), so \(\lambda=2\), \(x=(4,2)\), and the area is \(8\ \text{m}^2\).

Why it's a max: it's the only critical point, and the area is 0 at either end (\(x_0=0\) or \(x_1=0\)).

HW8 P1 ii · The closest point on a line (the general pattern)

\(f=\|x-p\|^2\), \(g=w\cdot x+b\). \(\nabla f=2(x-p)=\lambda w\), so \(x=p+\tfrac{\lambda}{2}w\): you move from \(p\) straight along \(w\), perpendicular to the line. Put that into \(g=0\):

\[ w\cdot p+\tfrac{\lambda}{2}\,w\cdot w+b=0\ \Rightarrow\ \lambda=\frac{-2(w\cdot p+b)}{w\cdot w},\qquad x=p-\frac{w\cdot p+b}{w\cdot w}\,w,\qquad \text{dist}=\frac{|w\cdot p+b|}{\|w\|} \]

With \(p=(5,5)\), \(w=(1,2)\), \(b=-5\): \(w\cdot p+b=10\) and \(w\cdot w=5\), so \(\lambda=-4\), \(x=(5,5)-2(1,2)=(3,1)\), and the distance is \(10/\sqrt5=2\sqrt5\approx4.47\).

The bridge to SVMs

\(\dfrac{|w\cdot p+b|}{\|w\|}\) is exactly the SVM margin formula. Lagrange and the "margin of \(x\)" are the same calculation. That's why this lesson came right before SVMs.

HW8 P2 · Matrix form

\(f=x^\top A x+x^\top(5,6)+123\) with \(A=\begin{bmatrix}1&2\\3&4\end{bmatrix}\), and \(g=(2,1)\cdot x\).

\(\nabla f=(A+A^\top)x+(5,6)=\begin{bmatrix}2&5\\5&8\end{bmatrix}x+\begin{bmatrix}5\\6\end{bmatrix}\). \(\nabla g=(2,1)\).

Trick: use the constraint first. \(x_1=-2x_0\) turns both rows into one-variable equations: \(-8x_0+5=2\lambda\) and \(-11x_0+6=\lambda\). So \(-8x_0+5=-22x_0+12\), giving \(x_0=\tfrac12\), \(x=(\tfrac12,-1)\), and \(\lambda=\tfrac12\).

Watch out

\(A\) is not symmetric, so \(A+A^\top\ne 2A\). Here \(2A\) would wrongly give \(\begin{bmatrix}2&4\\6&8\end{bmatrix}\). The constant 123 disappears from \(\nabla f\).

With a quadratic constraint (for example a circle), you can get several solutions. Work case by case, and throw out any that aren't on the constraint (Day 15's \(x_1=0\) case).

Lesson 3 · Intuition

SVM geometry: the widest street

  • Labels \(\pm1\) give one test for "correct": \(y_i(w\cdot x_i+b)\ge0\). A matching sign means the classification is correct.
  • Many lines separate the data (the perceptron stops at any one of them). The SVM picks the one whose closest sample is as far away as possible, so no sample is a close call.
  • \(w\) is perpendicular to the boundary. For two points on the line, \(w\cdot(x_A-x_B)=0\). This is how you get \(w\)'s direction in HW8 P3 iii.
  • Scaling \(w\) and \(b\) together doesn't move the line. The SVM picks the scale where the closest samples have \(y(w\cdot x+b)=1\). Then each edge is \(1/\|w\|\) away, the street is \(2/\|w\|\) wide, and maximizing the street means minimizing \(\|w\|\):
\[ \min_{w,b}\ \tfrac12\|w\|^2\quad\text{s.t.}\quad y_i(w\cdot x_i+b)\ge1\ \text{ for every } i \]
Watch out

\(y_i(w\cdot x_i+b)\) is a scaled score, not a distance. The distance (the margin) is that score divided by \(\|w\|\).

Quick check (Day 16 ICA)

The line has \(w=(3,4)\) and \(b=-5\). What's the margin of \((2,-1)\)?

Answer

\(\|w\|=5\). \(w\cdot x+b=6-4-5=-3\), so the margin is \(-3/5=-0.6\): it's on the side \(w\) points away from (\(\hat y=-1\)). Doubling \(w\) and \(b\) changes neither the line nor the margin.

Lesson 4 · HW8 P3

An SVM by hand: guess, solve, check

Lagrange applied to the support vectors \(S\) gives three facts:

\[ (1)\ w=\sum_{i\in S}\alpha_i y_i x_i \qquad (2)\ \sum_{i\in S}\alpha_i y_i=0 \qquad (3)\ y_i(w\cdot x_i+b)=1 \text{ for } i\in S \]
What each one means

(1) \(w\) is built only from the support vectors: add the + ones, subtract the − ones. Move any other sample (off the street) and nothing changes.

(2) The + side and the − side pull with equal total \(\alpha\).

(3) Every support vector sits exactly on its edge of the street.

The algorithm

Guess \(S\) (the samples nearest the gap). Solve: with 3 support vectors in 2-D, (3) alone gives \(w\) and \(b\) (3 equations, 3 unknowns), then (1) and (2) give the \(\alpha\)'s. Check: every sample has \(y(w\cdot x+b)\ge1\) and every \(\alpha\ge0\). If either fails, the guess was wrong.

The 2-support-vector shortcut (HW8 P3 i)

With one + sample and one −, (2) forces \(\alpha_+=\alpha_-=\alpha\), so \(w=\alpha(x_+-x_-)\). The boundary is the perpendicular bisector of the pair:

\[ w=\frac{2(x_+-x_-)}{\|x_+-x_-\|^2},\qquad \alpha=\frac{2}{\|x_+-x_-\|^2} \]

For \(B=(1,3)\) and \(D=(1,1)\): \(x_+-x_-=(0,2)\), so \(w=(0,1)\) and \(\alpha=\tfrac12\). From B on its edge: \(3+b=1\), so \(b=-2\). The boundary is \(x_1=2\).

HW8 P3. Filled = +1, open = −1. Shaded: the true street (A, B, D on its edges). Dashed: guess i, which puts A on the wrong side.

Why the closest pair failed (HW8 P3 ii)

For \(x_1=2\), A=(4,0) has \(y(w\cdot x+b)=1\cdot(0-2)=-2\), so it's misclassified. A sits low and to the right, "behind" the − class's height. Before you commit to a guess, look for a sample that a line through the closest pair would cut off.

HW8 P3 iii · S = {A, B, D}

  1. A and B are both on the + edge, so \(w\cdot(x_A-x_B)=0\): \(w\cdot(3,-3)=0\), so \(w=s(1,1)\).
  2. On the edges: A gives \(4s+b=1\). D gives \(-(2s+b)=1\). So \(s=1\), \(b=-3\), and \(w=(1,1)\).
  3. The \(\alpha\)'s: (2) gives \(\alpha_D=\alpha_A+\alpha_B\). (1) gives \(\alpha_A(4,0)+\alpha_B(1,3)-\alpha_D(1,1)=(3\alpha_A,\ 2\alpha_B-\alpha_A)=(1,1)\). So \(\alpha_A=\tfrac13\), \(\alpha_B=\tfrac23\), \(\alpha_D=1\).
  4. Check: C, E, F score 3, 3, 2 (all \(\ge1\)), and every \(\alpha\ge0\). This is the SVM. The street is \(2/\sqrt2=\sqrt2\approx1.41\) wide.
A sanity check I derived (not from class)

For a hard margin, \(\sum_i\alpha_i=\|w\|^2\). (Dot (1) with \(w\), then use (3) and (2).) In HW8: \(\tfrac13+\tfrac23+1=2=\|(1,1)\|^2\). In part i: \(\tfrac12+\tfrac12=1=\|(0,1)\|^2\). It's a 10-second check of your arithmetic. Don't present it as a required step.

Watch out

A negative \(\alpha\) means that sample "wants off" the edge: drop it from \(S\) and re-guess. A violated constraint means a sample you left out belongs in \(S\).

Lesson 5 · HW8 P3 ii and P4 (the notebook)

Slack, the four cases, and choosing C

When no line separates the classes, give each sample a slack \(\xi_i\ge0\): how far it falls short of its edge. At the optimum \(\xi_i=\max(0,\,1-y_i(w\cdot x_i+b))\).

Case\(y(w\cdot x+b)\)\(\xi\)MeaningSupport vector?
out\(\gt 1\)0correct, outside the streetno, \(\alpha=0\)

HW8 P3 ii (line \(x_1=2\)): A scores \(-2\), so \(\xi=3\) (wrong). B, C, D, F are on (\(\xi=0\)). E is out (score 2). The street is \(2/\|w\|=2\) wide. It's not the hard-margin SVM, because a hard margin allows no slack.

Prof. Higger (Piazza @55)

Study the margin size and the number of support vectors. All else equal, prefer a smaller C: more support vectors means the boundary is built from more samples, so it's a "simpler" model, much like choosing a larger \(k\) in k-NN.

HW8 notebook result (breast cancer, 30 features): cross-validated accuracy is 0.652 at \(C=10^{-4}\) (422 of 569 samples are support vectors), peaks at 0.975 at \(C=0.1\) (60 support vectors), and slips to 0.956 at \(C=1000\) (32 support vectors). Pick \(C=0.1\): it's tied for best, and it's the smaller C, so the wider street. Choose C by cross-validation, never by training accuracy, because training accuracy only improves as C grows.

Say it in two sentences (practice out loud)

Why does accuracy fall at small C? At large C?

Small C: slack is nearly free, so the street is so wide that most samples sit inside it. The boundary is averaged over almost everything and barely uses the data, so it underfits.

Large C: slack is expensive, so the boundary contorts to keep a few hard training samples correct. That doesn't carry over to held-out data, so it overfits.

Problem 1 · FAMILIAR · Lagrange

20 pts (10, 10)

i. A rectangular garden bed has sides \(x_0\) and \(x_1\). Edging costs \$2/m on the \(x_0\) side and \$3/m on the \(x_1\) side, and you spend exactly \$12: \(g(x)=2x_0+3x_1-12=0\). Use Lagrange multipliers to maximize the area \(f(x)=x_0x_1\). Give \(x\), \(\lambda\) and the area.

ii. Find the point on the line \(x_0-x_1=1\) closest to \(p=(3,0)\). Give \(x\), \(\lambda\) and the distance. Then compute the margin \(\frac{w\cdot p+b}{\|w\|}\) of \(p\) for \(w=(1,-1)\), \(b=-1\), and compare it to the distance.

Solution 1

i. \(\nabla f=(x_1,x_0)=\lambda(2,3)\), so \(x_1=2\lambda\) and \(x_0=3\lambda\). The constraint gives \(6\lambda+6\lambda=12\), so \(\lambda=1\). Then \(x=(3,2)\) and the area is \(6\). It's a max because the area is 0 at either end of the constraint.

ii. \(\nabla f=2(x-p)=\lambda w\), so \(x=p+\tfrac{\lambda}{2}(1,-1)=(3+\tfrac\lambda2,\ -\tfrac\lambda2)\). The constraint gives \(3+\tfrac\lambda2+\tfrac\lambda2-1=0\), so \(\lambda=-2\) and \(x=(2,1)\). Check: \(2-1=1\). The distance is \(\|(-1,1)\|=\sqrt2\).

Margin: \(w\cdot p+b=3-0-1=2\), so the margin is \(2/\sqrt2=\sqrt2\), the same as the distance. The margin formula is the closest-point distance, signed. (It's positive because \(p\) is on the side \(w\) points to.)

Problem 1 · FAMILIAR · Lagrange in matrix form

20 pts (12, 8)

i. Find every critical point of \(f(x)=x^\top\begin{bmatrix}2&1\\3&1\end{bmatrix}x+x^\top\begin{bmatrix}1\\-2\end{bmatrix}+7\) subject to \(g(x)=\begin{bmatrix}1\\1\end{bmatrix}\cdot x=0\), and give \(\lambda\) at each.

ii. Find the max and the min of \(f(x)=x_0+2x_1\) on the circle \(x_0^2+x_1^2=5\). Give \(\lambda\) at each, and say how you know which is which.

Solution 1

i. \(A+A^\top=\begin{bmatrix}4&4\\4&2\end{bmatrix}\), so \(\nabla f=(4x_0+4x_1+1,\ 4x_0+2x_1-2)\) and \(\nabla g=(1,1)\). The constraint gives \(x_1=-x_0\), so \(\nabla f=(1,\ 2x_0-2)=\lambda(1,1)\). That gives \(\lambda=1\), then \(2x_0-2=1\), so \(x=(\tfrac32,-\tfrac32)\). It's the only critical point.

ii. \(g=x_0^2+x_1^2-5\) and \(\nabla g=(2x_0,2x_1)\). \((1,2)=\lambda(2x_0,2x_1)\) gives \(x=(\tfrac1{2\lambda},\tfrac1\lambda)\). On the circle, \(\tfrac{1}{4\lambda^2}+\tfrac1{\lambda^2}=5\), so \(\lambda^2=\tfrac14\) and \(\lambda=\pm\tfrac12\).

\(\lambda=\tfrac12\): \(x=(1,2)\), \(f=5\), the max. \(\lambda=-\tfrac12\): \(x=(-1,-2)\), \(f=-5\), the min. Compare the values of \(f\) at the candidates: the circle is closed and bounded, so the largest value is the max. (\(\lambda\)'s sign shows whether \(\nabla f\) points outward along \(\nabla g\) or inward; here outward is uphill.)

Blank-page drill (write these from memory)

  1. The two Lagrange equations, and why \(\nabla f\parallel\nabla g\) (one sentence).
  2. \(\nabla\|x-p\|^2\), \(\nabla(x^\top Ax)\), and the closest point / distance to \(w\cdot x+b=0\).

Mistakes that cost points

  • Using \(2A\) instead of \(A+A^\top\) for a non-symmetric \(A\).
  • Forgetting to substitute back into \(g(x)=0\). \(\nabla f=\lambda\nabla g\) alone doesn't give \(\lambda\).
  • Writing \(g\) without the "\(=0\)" form (for example \(b=+5\) instead of \(-5\)). Check the sign of \(b\).
  • Thinking \(\lambda\) must be positive. Only the SVM \(\alpha\)'s must be \(\ge0\).
  • Dropping \(y_i\) for the − samples: (3) for a − sample is \(-(w\cdot x+b)=1\).
  • Stopping after the solve. Without the check (every score \(\ge1\), every \(\alpha\ge0\)), you lose the "is this the SVM?" points.
  • Saying the street is \(1/\|w\|\) wide. That's half of it; the width is \(2/\|w\|\).
  • Saying "pick the C with the best training accuracy". Use cross-validation.
  • Not showing your work. The quiz requires it.
