The written HW8 problems: Lagrange (P1, P2) and an SVM by hand (P3), plus being able to explain what C does. No code.

  • Expect two problems of about 20 points each: one FAMILIAR (an HW8 problem with new numbers) and one FRESH (the same idea in a new setup). Show your work.
  • You get a reminder sheet (Piazza @53). It has the Lagrange equations and the SVM formulas, so you don't need to memorize those.

Four things the sheet does NOT give you

Street width\(\dfrac{2}{\|w\|}\)
Slack\(\xi=\max(0,\ 1-\text{score})\). A point's score is \(y(w\cdot x+b)\).
Gradient of a distance²\(\nabla\|x-p\|^2=2(x-p)\)
"Is this the SVM?"every score \(\ge1\) and every \(\alpha\ge0\)
See the full reminder sheet (Piazza @53)

Gradient rules: \(\nabla(a^\top x)=a,\ \nabla(x^\top A x)=(A+A^\top)x\)

Lagrange: a max or min of \(f(x)\) subject to \(g(x)=0\) solves \(\nabla f=\lambda\nabla g\) and \(g(x)=0\).

SVM: labels \(\pm1\); \(\hat y=\operatorname{sign}(w\cdot x+b)\); the margin of \(x\) is \(\frac{w\cdot x+b}{\|w\|}\); the street's edges are \(w\cdot x+b=\pm1\).

Soft margin: \(\min\ \tfrac12\|w\|^2+C\sum\xi_i\) subject to \(y_i(w\cdot x_i+b)\ge1-\xi_i,\ \xi_i\ge0\).

Solution: \(w=\sum\alpha_iy_ix_i,\ \sum\alpha_iy_i=0,\ 0\le\alpha_i\le C\), and \(\alpha_i=0\) unless \(y_i(w\cdot x_i+b)\le1\).

Lesson 1

Lagrange: the best point on a path

You want the highest (or lowest) value of something, but you have to stay on a path. The best spot is where the path just touches a contour line instead of crossing it.

HW8 P1 i, the fence. Green line = the path you must stay on, \(x_0+2x_1=8\). Curves = equal-area contours. Purple arrow = the direction the area grows fastest. Green arrow = the direction that leaves the fence path. Where they line up, you can't do better.

Why "the arrows line up" means "best"

  • The purple arrow (\(\nabla f\)) points where the area grows fastest.
  • At (2, 3) the purple arrow leans along the path. So sliding along the fence still increases the area: not the best spot yet.
  • At (4, 2) the purple arrow points straight off the path, exactly like the green arrow (\(\nabla g\)). Sliding either way can't help. That's the best spot.

"Same direction" in math: one arrow is a multiple of the other. That multiple is \(\lambda\). It's just a number, and it can be negative (arrows pointing opposite ways also count).

On the quiz

  1. Write what you maximize, \(f\), and the path as \(g(x)=0\) (move everything to one side).
  2. Take both gradients (the arrows).
  3. Set \(\nabla f=\lambda\nabla g\) and solve for \(x\) in terms of \(\lambda\).
  4. Put that \(x\) into \(g(x)=0\) to find \(\lambda\). Then you have \(x\).
HW8 P1 i, written out

Maximize area \(f=x_0x_1\) on the fence \(g=x_0+2x_1-8=0\).

\(\nabla f=(x_1,\ x_0)\) and \(\nabla g=(1,\ 2)\).

\((x_1,x_0)=\lambda(1,2)\) gives \(x_1=\lambda\) and \(x_0=2\lambda\).

On the fence: \(2\lambda+2\lambda=8\), so \(\lambda=2\), \(x=(4,2)\), and the area is \(8\ \text{m}^2\).

Prof. Higger (Nov 3): keep letters like \(w\), \(b\), \(p\) until the end and plug numbers in last. It's less writing and fewer slips.

Watch out: step 3 alone never gives \(\lambda\). You always need step 4.

Lesson 2

The two other Lagrange problems in HW8

A. The closest point on a line (P1 ii)

From a point, the shortest way to a line is straight across, at a right angle.

The dashed path runs in the direction \(w=(1,2)\), the line's "perpendicular" direction.

  1. \(f=\|x-p\|^2\) (the squared distance is easier to differentiate and has the same best point). \(g=w\cdot x+b\).
  2. \(\nabla f=2(x-p)\) and \(\nabla g=w\). Setting \(2(x-p)=\lambda w\) gives \(x=p+\tfrac{\lambda}{2}w\). In words: start at \(p\) and move along \(w\).
  3. Put that into the line to get \(\lambda\), then \(x\).
HW8 P1 ii

\(x=(5+\tfrac\lambda2,\ 5+\lambda)\). On the line: \(5+\tfrac\lambda2+2(5+\lambda)-5=0\), so \(\lambda=-4\).

\(x=(3,1)\). Distance \(=\|(3,1)-(5,5)\|=\sqrt{4+16}=\sqrt{20}\approx4.47\).

Why this matters for SVMs: the shortcut answer is \(\text{distance}=\dfrac{|w\cdot p+b|}{\|w\|}\). Here that's \(10/\sqrt5=\sqrt{20}\). That is exactly the SVM "margin of a point" on your sheet.

B. Matrix form (P2)

Same recipe. The only new skill is taking the gradient of \(x^\top Ax\), and the sheet gives you that rule.

  1. Gradient: \(\nabla f=(A+A^\top)x+\text{(the linear part's vector)}\). The constant (123) disappears.
  2. Use the constraint to replace one variable first. It turns everything into one-variable equations.
  3. Match the two rows of \(\nabla f=\lambda\nabla g\), then solve.
HW8 P2

\(A+A^\top=\begin{bmatrix}1&2\\3&4\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}1&3\\2&4\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}2&5\\5&8\end{bmatrix}\), so \(\nabla f=(2x_0+5x_1+5,\ 5x_0+8x_1+6)\) and \(\nabla g=(2,1)\).

The constraint \(2x_0+x_1=0\) gives \(x_1=-2x_0\). The rows become \(-8x_0+5=2\lambda\) and \(-11x_0+6=\lambda\).

Substituting the second into the first: \(-8x_0+5=-22x_0+12\), so \(x_0=\tfrac12\). That gives \(x=(\tfrac12,-1)\) and \(\lambda=\tfrac12\).

Watch out: \(A\) isn't symmetric, so \(A+A^\top\) is not \(2A\). Write out \(A^\top\) and add.

Lesson 3

SVM: the widest street

Draw the widest possible street between the two groups, with no point standing in it. The line down the middle is the classifier.

HW8 P3. Filled = +1, open = −1. Green band = the widest street. A, B and D touch its edges: those are the support vectors.

Four facts, in words

  • Score of a point: \(y(w\cdot x+b)\). Positive means it's on the correct side. The street's edges are where the score is exactly 1.
  • Width of the street: \(2/\|w\|\). So a shorter \(w\) means a wider street. That's why the SVM minimizes \(\|w\|\).
  • The direction \(w\) points straight across the street, perpendicular to the middle line.
  • Only the points touching the street matter. Move any other point (without entering the street) and nothing changes.

Watch out: the score isn't a distance. The real distance from the middle line is score \(\div\|w\|\). That's the "margin" on your sheet.

Lesson 4

Finding the street by hand (HW8 P3)

Guess which points touch the street. Make each of them score exactly 1, solve, then check that nobody else is in the street.

  1. Guess the touching points from a quick sketch.
  2. Touching equations: each guessed point has score exactly 1. With 3 points in 2-D, these alone give \(w\) and \(b\).
  3. The \(\alpha\)'s (how hard each point pushes) come from the two sheet facts: \(w=\sum\alpha_iy_ix_i\) and \(\sum\alpha_iy_i=0\).
  4. Check: every other point scores \(\ge1\), and every \(\alpha\ge0\). If either fails, the guess was wrong.

Part i: guess only the closest pair, B and D

With one point from each side, the street is just centered between them, and \(w\) points from D to B.

Shortcut for two touching points

\(w=\dfrac{2(x_+-x_-)}{\|x_+-x_-\|^2}\). Here \(x_B-x_D=(0,2)\), so \(w=\tfrac24(0,2)=(0,1)\).

B scores 1: \(0\cdot1+1\cdot3+b=1\), so \(b=-2\). The middle line is \(x_1=2\). Both \(\alpha\)'s are \(\tfrac12\).

Part ii, the check: A=(4,0) scores \(1\cdot(0-2)=-2\). It's on the wrong side, so this guess fails. (In the picture, the dashed red line cuts A off.) Lesson: look for a point the closest-pair line would cut off.

Part iii: guess A, B and D

Solve

Direction: A and B touch the same edge, so the edge runs from A to B. \(w\) is perpendicular to \(A-B=(3,-3)\), so \(w=s(1,1)\).

Touching: A: \(4s+b=1\). D (a − point, so flip the sign): \(-(2s+b)=1\). Together: \(s=1\), \(b=-3\), \(w=(1,1)\).

\(\alpha\)'s: \(\alpha_A(4,0)+\alpha_B(1,3)-\alpha_D(1,1)=(1,1)\) and \(\alpha_A+\alpha_B=\alpha_D\). That gives \(\alpha_A=\tfrac13\), \(\alpha_B=\tfrac23\), \(\alpha_D=1\).

Check: C, E and F score 3, 3 and 2 (all \(\ge1\)), and every \(\alpha\) is positive. This is the SVM. Width \(=2/\sqrt2\approx1.41\).

Watch out: for a − point, the touching equation is \(-(w\cdot x+b)=1\). Forgetting the minus sign is the most common slip.

Lesson 5

Slack and C: when the groups overlap

If no clean street exists, let some points cheat by standing in the street or on the wrong side. Slack is how far a point cheats. C is the fine per unit of cheating.

Where the point isScoreSlack \(\xi\)Touches the street?
out: correct side, outside the streetmore than 10no
on: exactly on its edgeexactly 10yes
in: correct side, but inside the street0 to 10 to 1yes
wrong: wrong side of the middle linebelow 0more than 1yes

Slack \(=\max(0,\ 1-\text{score})\). In HW8 P3 ii, A scores −2, so its slack is 3 and it's "wrong".

What C does

Small C (cheating is cheap)Large C (cheating is expensive)
Streetwidenarrow
Points touching the streetmanyfew
Risktoo simple (underfits)bends to a few odd points (overfits)

Prof. Higger, Piazza @55: know how C changes the street width and the number of support vectors. All else equal, prefer the smaller C: more points shape the line, so it's simpler, like picking a bigger \(k\) in k-NN.

HW8 notebook result: cross-validated accuracy was low at tiny C, peaked around C = 0.1, and dipped slightly at huge C. Pick C = 0.1: it's tied for best and it's the smaller C.

The two sentences to say if asked "why does accuracy drop at small C and at large C?"

Small C: cheating is almost free, so the street is so wide that most points are inside it and the line barely uses the data. It underfits.

Large C: cheating is expensive, so the line twists to get a few hard training points right. That doesn't carry over to new data. It overfits. Choose C by cross-validation.

Problem 1 · Lagrange

20 pts

i. Edging costs \$2/m on the \(x_0\) sides and \$3/m on the \(x_1\) sides, and you spend exactly \$12: \(2x_0+3x_1=12\). Maximize the area \(x_0x_1\). Give \(x\), \(\lambda\) and the area.

ii. Find the point on the line \(x_0-x_1=1\) closest to \(p=(3,0)\). Give \(x\), \(\lambda\) and the distance.

Solution

i. \((x_1,x_0)=\lambda(2,3)\) gives \(x_1=2\lambda\) and \(x_0=3\lambda\). Then \(6\lambda+6\lambda=12\), so \(\lambda=1\), \(x=(3,2)\), and the area is 6.

ii. \(w=(1,-1)\), \(b=-1\). \(x=p+\tfrac\lambda2w=(3+\tfrac\lambda2,\ -\tfrac\lambda2)\). On the line: \(3+\lambda-1=0\), so \(\lambda=-2\) and \(x=(2,1)\). The distance is \(\|(-1,1)\|=\sqrt2\). Check with the shortcut: \(|3-0-1|/\sqrt2=\sqrt2\).

ii. The scores are P 1, Q 3, R 5, S \(-1\), T 1, U 1. S is wrong (slack 2), so the guess fails. A sketch shows S=(1,4) sitting to the right of the line, on the + side.

Practice Quiz 8B

Problem 1 · Lagrange

20 pts

i. Find the critical point of \(f(x)=x^\top\begin{bmatrix}2&1\\3&1\end{bmatrix}x+x^\top\begin{bmatrix}1\\-2\end{bmatrix}+7\) on the line \(x_0+x_1=0\), and give \(\lambda\).

ii. On the circle \(x_0^2+x_1^2=5\), find where \(x_0+2x_1\) is largest and where it's smallest.

Solution

i. \(A+A^\top=\begin{bmatrix}4&4\\4&2\end{bmatrix}\), so \(\nabla f=(4x_0+4x_1+1,\ 4x_0+2x_1-2)\) and \(\nabla g=(1,1)\). With \(x_1=-x_0\): \((1,\ 2x_0-2)=\lambda(1,1)\). So \(\lambda=1\) and \(x_0=\tfrac32\), giving \(x=(\tfrac32,-\tfrac32)\).

ii. \(\nabla g=(2x_0,2x_1)\). \((1,2)=\lambda(2x_0,2x_1)\) gives \(x=(\tfrac1{2\lambda},\tfrac1\lambda)\). On the circle, \(\tfrac5{4\lambda^2}=5\), so \(\lambda=\pm\tfrac12\). That gives two candidates, \((1,2)\) with value 5 (the largest) and \((-1,-2)\) with value −5 (the smallest). Plug in and compare.
