Binary classification: name the four boxes

A sample is one example, such as one transaction. A feature is information the model receives. The target is the known answer. Binary classification predicts one of two categories. Choose which category counts as positive before computing anything. Here positive means fraud, and a positive prediction means the alarm rings.

Predict negative: no alarmPredict positive: alarm
Truth negative: legitimateTN: correctly quietFP: false alarm
Truth positive: fraudFN: missed fraudTP: fraud caught

The first word says whether the prediction is correct. The second says what the model predicted. A confusion matrix is just these four counts. This guide now follows HW5: truth in rows and prediction in columns. Other sources may transpose it, so read the labels every time.

N=TP+FN+FP+TN,P=TP+FN,N−=FP+TNN=TP+FN+FP+TN,\qquad P=TP+FN,\qquad N_-=FP+TNN=TP+FN+FP+TN,P=TP+FN,N−​=FP+TN

P counts actual positives; N₋ counts actual negatives. Their totals are fixed while you move a prediction threshold on the same dataset.

Explain without abbreviations

A missed fraud is a false negative because the alarm predicted negative even though fraud was present. A false alarm is a false positive because the alarm predicted positive when fraud was absent.

Which denominator belongs to precision?

Zero denominators and F1

If there are no predicted positives, precision is undefined as a fraction; some software deliberately reports 0 or NaN. State the convention. If no actual positives exist, TPR is undefined. F1 is a Day 9 extension, not one of HW5’s named five metrics: it is 2TP/(2TP+FP+FN), the harmonic mean of precision and recall. In this example F1 = 60/80 = 0.75.

Lazy alarm: why accuracy alone fails

Rarer disease: separate ranking from accuracy

The key says ROC stays the same while the accuracy-favoring threshold increases. The assumption is that class-conditional score behavior stays fixed and only the disease prior changes. ROC uses rates within each truth group. Accuracy weights those rates by how common each truth group is.

Acc(t;π)=πTPR(t)+(1−π)(1−FPR(t))Acc(t;\pi)=\pi TPR(t)+(1-\pi)(1-FPR(t))Acc(t;π)=πTPR(t)+(1−π)(1−FPR(t))

For a discrete list, the best threshold may stay in the same interval until the prior changes enough; it need not strictly rise for every tiny prior change. Reweighting the official table at π=0.5 favors t=0.6, with accuracy 0.75. At π=0.01 it favors t=0.9, with accuracy 0.9925. This calculation holds the table’s conditional rates fixed; it does not invent new patient labels.

Loss, slope, and gradient from the beginning

A parameter is a number the model learns. A loss function assigns a number to a parameter setting: smaller means better fit under that loss. Optimization means searching for a setting with low loss. A derivative is the local slope of a function of one number.

For a parameter w, a positive slope says increasing w locally increases the loss. To decrease loss, move left. A negative slope says move right. A slope of zero is a stationary point; it could be a minimum, maximum, or neither.

f(w)=(w−3)2,f′(w)=2(w−3),f′(0)=−6f(w)=(w-3)^2,\quad f\prime(w)=2(w-3),\quad f\prime(0)=-6f(w)=(w−3)2,f′(w)=2(w−3),f′(0)=−6

At w = 0 the slope is −6, so moving right a little lowers the loss. “Negative slope” does not mean “make w more negative.” You move against the slope.

A partial derivative measures change in one parameter while holding the others fixed. A gradient stacks the partial derivatives into a vector. A vector is an ordered list of numbers. Its norm is its length.

∇f(w)=[∂f/∂w0∂f/∂w1],∥v∥2=v⋅v=∑jvj2\nabla f(w)=\begin{bmatrix}\partial f/\partial w_0\\\partial f/\partial w_1\end{bmatrix},\qquad \|v\|^2=v\cdot v=\sum_jv_j^2∇f(w)=[∂f/∂w0​∂f/∂w1​​],∥v∥2=v⋅v=j∑​vj2​

Step size, convergence, and local minima

Convergence means the iterates approach a stable solution. A step size can be too small and make progress slow, or too large and make the iterates overshoot and diverge. These statements can be made exact for the toy bowl below.

f(w)=(w−3)2,ek=wk−3,ek+1=(1−2η)ekf(w)=(w-3)^2,\quad e_k=w_k-3,\quad e_{k+1}=(1-2\eta)e_kf(w)=(w−3)2,ek​=wk​−3,ek+1​=(1−2η)ek​
Learning rate for this specific functionBehavior away from the optimum
0 < η < 0.5Approaches 3 from one side
η = 0.5Reaches 3 in one step
0.5 < η < 1Alternates around 3 with shrinking error
η = 1Bounces with constant error
η > 1Alternates with growing error
η = 0Does not move

These numerical boundaries belong to this function, not every loss. Multiplying a loss by a positive constant multiplies its gradient and changes the stable learning-rate range.

What “minimum” really means

A local minimum is best among nearby parameter settings. A global minimum is best over the whole allowed domain. A convex function has no local minimum worse than a global one; the line between any two graph points lies on or above the graph. For a differentiable convex function, a zero gradient identifies a global minimizer. A suitable learning rate and other conditions still matter for GD convergence.

For a nonconvex loss, different starts can lead to different outcomes. Restarts may help find a better solution but do not certify a global minimum. Even a zero gradient may be a maximum or saddle point. Stopping when the gradient norm is small is a practical rule, not a proof of global optimality.

Fresh questions: what changes and why?

Original transfer practice, not leaked or predicted quiz questions. For every story, write four short lines: what stays fixed → what changes → the relevant formula → why the result follows. A correct number with an unexplained method is unfinished.

1. A bank gets ten times fewer frauds

Keep class-conditional alarm rates fixed. What happens to prior, TPR, FPR, precision, and accuracy?

Reveal reasoning

Prior falls; TPR/FPR stay fixed by assumption. Precision generally falls when TPR and FPR are positive. Accuracy follows π·TPR+(1−π)(1−FPR); its direction depends on the rates. In the official HW5 example it rises because 1−FPR exceeds TPR. Do not claim that lower prior always improves accuracy.

2. Duplicate the whole test dataset

Duplicate every labeled score exactly once. What changes?

Reveal reasoning

Every confusion-matrix count doubles. All five ratios and AUC stay the same, when defined. Doubling the rows does not create independent new evidence.

3. Replace score s by 3s+7

What happens to ROC-AUC? How do you preserve the decisions from threshold t?

Reveal reasoning

Ranking and AUC stay unchanged. Use threshold 3t+7: 3s+7 ≥ 3t+7 exactly when s ≥ t. Leaving the threshold unchanged can alter decisions.

4. Reverse a weak ranking

AUC is 0.3. Replace each score by its negative. What changes and why?

Reveal reasoning

The ranking reverses, so AUC becomes 0.7 with half credit for ties. This does not say every possible threshold now gives a good operational tradeoff.

5. Multiply the entire loss by 10

Keep the same start. How do you get exactly the old GD path?

Reveal reasoning

The gradient becomes 10 times larger; divide η by 10. The minimizers stay the same for positive scaling. A larger loss scale is not evidence of a worse parameter setting.

6. Translate the quadratic

For f(w)=‖w−b‖²+a·w, find the stationary point and explain whether it is a global minimum.

Reveal reasoning

Gradient = 2(w−b)+a. Setting it to zero gives w*=b−a/2. This is a convex quadratic with positive curvature in every direction, so that stationary point is the unique global minimum.

7. A threshold looks best on training data

You test 50 thresholds on training scores and report the best accuracy as future performance. What is wrong?

Reveal reasoning

Training predictions can be optimistic, and choosing the best among many settings adds selection optimism. Choose the threshold on validation data or within a proper CV procedure; use independent data to estimate the selected procedure’s performance. Specify the cost tradeoff, not accuracy alone.

8. Zero gradient at the wrong place

At w=0, the gradient of f(w)=−w² is zero. Does GD prove a minimum?

Reveal reasoning

No: f has a maximum there and no finite global minimum on the real line. Starting exactly at zero yields no update. Check the function’s shape and assumptions before interpreting a small gradient.
